6.1 What Is an Ablation Test?

In simulation and machine learning research, an ablation test is the standard methodology for isolating the contribution of individual model components: disable one component, re-run the full experiment under otherwise identical conditions, and measure what changes (Sargent 2020). Components that are load-bearing will produce meaningful shifts in output when removed; components that are incidental will leave the results largely intact. This section applies that methodology to two specific mechanisms in the H1 agent-based model — knowledge decay and source asymmetry — to determine whether the H1 ordering result is a genuine property of the sharing-scope architecture or an artifact of those auxiliary design choices.

The component being ablated in each experiment targets a distinct part of the agent learning pipeline. Knowledge decay (exp11) is a forgetting mechanism: by default, an agent's accumulated prevention knowledge erodes exponentially over time (decay rate = 0.001 per timestep), modeling staff turnover and practice atrophy. Source asymmetry (exp12) is a routing rule: when an incident occurs at Team A, Team A is excluded from stages 2–4 of the post-incident learning pipeline, on the premise that the source team already holds first-hand knowledge of the event. The diagnostic question for both ablations is the same: does the H1 ordering — NONE > LOCAL > NEIGHBOR > GLOBAL in cumulative incidents — emerge from the sharing-scope mechanism itself, or does it depend on one of these auxiliary components to hold? If removing decay collapses the ordering, decay is load-bearing. If the ordering survives, sharing scope alone is sufficient to produce it. Both experiments were run under the same conditions as the primary H1 experiment: 20 teams, Watts-Strogatz small-world network, 365 simulated days, 100 independent random seeds, all four scenarios.
