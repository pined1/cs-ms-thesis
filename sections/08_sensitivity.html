8. Sensitivity Sweeps — Which Model Knobs Matter?

8.1 Sensitivity Sweeps vs. Ablation Studies

An ablation study removes a component entirely to test whether it contributes at all. A sensitivity sweep keeps the component active but varies its numeric value across a wide range, asking whether the findings would change if the model had been calibrated differently.

8.2 Sweep 1 — Acquisition Probability (Stage 1 Gate)

What it is: The per-day probability that a team reads and registers a postmortem that has been shared into their knowledge pool. Default: 0.9. Range tested: 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 1.0.

The span of ~43 incidents (~11% variation) makes this moderately sensitive. The critical threshold appears between 0.3 and 0.5: below 0.5, teams miss enough postmortems that incident counts rise sharply (~39 additional incidents). Above 0.5, the daily retry mechanism compensates and raising the rate further yields diminishing returns — moving from 0.9 to perfect (1.0) saves only 6 more incidents. H1 ordering holds across the full range tested.

Stage 3 (transformation, gated by cosine similarity) is the true bottleneck: teams that cannot connect an incoming postmortem to their own systems never reach Stage 4 regardless of how willing they are to act. Raising exploitation probability has negligible effect when the pipeline is blocked upstream. This directly corroborates H3 — the bottleneck in this model is recognition capacity, not execution willingness.

8.6 Sweep 5 — Initial Knowledge State (Cold vs. Warm Start)

What it is: The default initializes all teams with zero prior knowledge (cold start). This sweep tests whether pre-existing organizational knowledge changes the findings.

ConditionRate-Adjusted Incidents/Day
Cold NEIGHBOR0.920
Warm GLOBAL → NEIGHBOR0.718
Warm LOCAL → LOCAL1.091

The warm GLOBAL start produced a 22% improvement in daily incident rate relative to the cold NEIGHBOR baseline. The warm LOCAL start performed worse than the cold baseline — teams accumulated narrow, domain-specific knowledge that does not transfer well across subsystems. The LOCAL warm start illustrates how a history of siloed learning can create the appearance of preparation without cross-domain breadth.

Implication for H1: The cold start default is deliberately conservative. Mature organizations with accumulated cross-team knowledge are closer to the warm GLOBAL condition. The 45% incident reduction observed in H1 may understate the advantage of GLOBAL sharing for organizations that have already invested in broad knowledge-sharing practices — though this remains a conjecture from the model's structure rather than an empirically verified claim.
