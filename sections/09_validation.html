9. Extended Validation Tests

Overview

Establishing H1 through H4, running ablations, and completing sensitivity sweeps provided a strong internal foundation. A final battery of nine validation tests were designed to stress test the model.

Test 1 — Time Dynamics: When Does H1 Ordering Emerge?

Question: Does the knowledge-sharing advantage manifest immediately, or is there a warmup period? Method: 50 seeds per scenario; incident counts extracted in non-overlapping 30-day windows.

Results: H1 ordering is present from day 1. In the first 30-day window: NONE = 40.3, LOCAL = 39.9, NEIGHBOR = 38.7, GLOBAL = 34.7. GLOBAL knowledge reaches 0.577 by day 30 and 0.992 by day 90, then plateaus. Conclusion: There is no warmup artifact.

Question: Would H1 collapse if the simulation ran longer? Method: 100 seeds at four durations (180, 365, 730, 1095 days); normalized to incidents per day.

Test 3 — Barabási-Albert ba_m Sweep

Question: At what connectivity level does BA match or exceed WS (336 incidents)? Results: ba_m = 1: 368 incidents. ba_m = 2: 347. ba_m = 3: 331 (crossover point). ba_m = 6: 305. Below ba_m = 3, hubs act as bottlenecks; above it, hubs become accelerators.

Test 4 — Watts-Strogatz ws_k Sweep

Question: Is the NEIGHBOR result sensitive to how many direct neighbors each team has? Results: ws_k = 2: 360.7. ws_k = 4 (default): 336.0. ws_k = 6: 312.8. ws_k = 8: 301.0. ws_k = 10: 288.2. The relationship is monotonically improving — more neighbors consistently means more learning. The default ws_k = 4 is conservative.

Test 7 — H3 500-Seed Rerun with Extended Parameter Range

Question: Does the diminishing-returns pattern in H3 hold under high-power conditions and across an extended parameter range? Result: The model's automated flag reported: "Diminishing returns detected (H3 supported)." The pattern is confirmed at 500-seed power with the extended range (0.0 to 0.5).
